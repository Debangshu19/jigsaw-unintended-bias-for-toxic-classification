9Cross-Script Transfer — RAVEN-X ★ (our original contribution)
On-slide content
  • The idea in one line: teach the model which words are hateful in English, and have that skill fire on Devanagari Hindi — where nobody labelled hateful words.
  • Method: one shared MuRIL encoder (236M) + two heads — Classify (Hindi HASOC + English) and Rationale (English only); masked multi-task loss → never trains on a missing label.
  • Why it works: MuRIL puts an English slur and a Hindi slur in nearby regions of one shared meaning space, even though they share zero subword tokens → transfer is through meaning, not spelling.
  • Results: macro-F1 0.842 vs TF-IDF baseline 0.787 (+5.5); English rationale token-F1 0.653 → 0.679.
  • Honest trade-off: the Hindi faithfulness objective traded 0.842→0.802 macro-F1 for better rationales — the accuracy-vs-explainability tension we set out to measure.
  • ✅ Now live in the product — our MuRIL model serves Devanagari through the same API. Live Hindi model (HASOC test set, seed 42): Accuracy 0.837 · Recall 0.819 · F1 0.842 · Macro-F1 0.837 · Precision 0.866. Real demo: तुम मादरचोद हो। → Toxic 0.84; बहुत बढ़िया लेख, धन्यवाद! → Safe.

11Summary & Future Scope (Ensembling)
On-slide content
Accomplished:
  • Competitive analysis — classical vs RNN vs transformer; proved recall is the honest metric.
  • DistilBERT — Recall 0.899, ROC-AUC 0.9515.
  • Cross-script transfer (RAVEN-X) — macro-F1 0.842 (+5.5), now live.
  • Full implementation — model → API → web + extension + mobile, language-aware routing.
Future scope — ensembling (to raise recall further):
  • Average probabilities of DistilBERT + a larger transformer + an RNN-family model.
  • Theory: K models variance σ² each → averaging keeps bias, cuts variance to σ²/K; with correlation ρ, variance → ρσ² + (1−ρ)σ²/K. Gain = diversity.
  • Proven: Kaggle Jigsaw 8th place = 4 diverse models by simple averaging; no top team used one model.
  • Also: multi-sample dropout (Inoue 2019); multi-seed runs; script-stratified faithfulness on Devanagari.
Say
"Our best single model already gives 0.90 recall. Ensembling diverse architectures provably reduces variance without adding bias — that is how we push recall higher. We report the number once measured, not before."

★Cross-Script Transfer — Deep Dive & Anticipated Questions
Rehearse these — the examiners will push hardest on the novel part.
Q. What exactly is your novel contribution?
Cross-script, word-level rationale transfer: we supervise "which words are hateful" only in English, and it works on Devanagari Hindi where there are no word-level labels — and we measure whether the transferred explanation is faithful, not assume it.
Q. How can you explain Hindi with zero Hindi explanation labels?
Masked multi-task loss. The rationale head's error only applies to English examples (which have rationales); the classifier's error applies to Hindi + English. So we never train on a label we don't have — the skill transfers through the shared encoder.
Q. Why is transfer even possible across different alphabets?
MuRIL is pre-trained jointly on Devanagari Hindi + English, so it aligns the two languages in one shared meaning space. An English slur and a Hindi slur land in nearby vector regions even though they share zero subword tokens — transfer is through meaning, not spelling.
Q. Why MuRIL and not mBERT / XLM-R?
MuRIL is trained specifically on 17 Indian languages + English, so it is stronger on Devanagari; mBERT/XLM-R are weaker on Indic scripts.
Q. How do you measure faithfulness with no labels?
Deletion test (ERASER: comprehensiveness / sufficiency / AOPC). Delete the highlighted words — if the toxic score collapses, the explanation was real. This is label-free, so it works in Hindi.
Q. Is your data really code-mixed / Hinglish?
We measured it: ~82% Devanagari characters, ~75% of posts Devanagari-dominant. So it is native-script Hindi, not romanized Hinglish; the small Latin slice we analyse separately.
Q. Isn't 4,665 Hindi posts too small?
That scarcity IS the low-resource problem — and the reason for transfer. If Hindi had 500k labels the project would be trivial. We borrow supervision from English precisely because Hindi labels are few.
Q. Did explainability cost accuracy?
Yes — about 4 macro-F1 points (0.842 → 0.802) when the faithfulness objective is on. We report it honestly; it is the accuracy-vs-explainability trade-off we pre-registered as a possible outcome.
Q. Why not just compare to your English DistilBERT (0.885)?
Different language, task, label scheme, and class balance — comparing them is a category error. The correct baseline is a TF-IDF model on the same Hindi split (0.787), which we beat by 5.5 points.
Q. Did you distil BERT yourself?
No. DistilBERT was already distilled by HuggingFace; we fine-tuned it. We never ran a teacher→student loop.

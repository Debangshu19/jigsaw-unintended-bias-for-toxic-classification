OUR ORIGINAL METHOD · LOW-RESOURCE HINDI

RAVEN-X — our original contribution (Hindi, cross-script rationale transfer)

Why we re-scoped the project

Our supervisor, Dr. Arpita Dutta, is second author of the survey "A Survey on Automatic Online Hate Speech Detection in Low-Resource Languages" (arXiv:2411.19017). That survey points to low-resource and code-mixed Hindi-English as the open gap. Building another English Jigsaw model is a reproduction, not a contribution. So she asked us to propose our own method in low-resource Hindi instead. RAVEN-X is that method.

Data truth — what we actually measured on disk

We checked the HASOC-2019 Hindi file ourselves (4,665 rows). It is native-script Hindi, not romanized Hinglish: measured by us 82.5% of script characters are Devanagari; 74.9% of posts are Devanagari-dominant and only 7.3% are Latin-dominant. This matters for the method: a Devanagari slur and its English equivalent share zero subword tokens. So any transfer from English to Hindi cannot ride on shared spelling. It has to pass through MuRIL's shared meaning space, purely as representation.

The official gold test set is blind, so we cannot use it. We froze a stratified split with seed=42 and report on it: train 3,265 / val 699 / test 701.

The method

One shared MuRIL-base encoder (12 layers, 768-d, ~236M params) feeds two heads:

CLASSIFY head

Toxic / not toxic. Supervised on Hindi HASOC and English data together.

RATIONALE head

Which words are toxic. 768→256→1 per word. Supervised only on English HateXplain — never on Hindi.

The two heads are tied together by a per-head-masked multi-task loss: each loss term applies only where its label exists, so we never train on a missing label. On top of that we add a label-free faithfulness self-objective on Hindi — comprehensiveness and sufficiency, measured by masking words at the attention level and re-running the full encoder. Each batch of 16 is mixed: 8 HASOC-Hindi + 8 HateXplain-English. AdamW, discriminative learning rates (2e-5 encoder / 1e-4 heads), max_len 128, fp16, on a T4 GPU.

Why this is novel — and the honest negative-result framing

The novelty is the conjunction of three things at once: cross-script (English → Devanagari Hindi), at the rationale level (word-by-word, not just a label), with zero target-language rationale labels. We test whether explanation faithfulness survives the script boundary with a label-free deletion test we built, not an assumption. If the answer turns out to be no — that transfer does not survive crossing into Devanagari — that is still a rigorous negative result the field does not currently have, and reporting it honestly is itself a contribution.

Takeaway. RAVEN-X is one MuRIL encoder with two heads and a masked multi-task loss. On our frozen split it reached macro-F1 0.842 on validation, +5.5 points over the TF-IDF baseline (0.787), and we directly measured the accuracy-versus-explainability trade-off the design predicted.
Honest caveat. This is a fast single-seed run; MuRIL validation was capped at 400 examples by the eval loop and the clean MuRIL test number is still pending. The headline result — whether faithful explanations transfer across script into Devanagari — is the pending Week-4 measurement, not yet done. And the English DistilBERT 0.885 is not a baseline here: it is a different language, task, label scheme, and class balance, so comparing the two would be a category error. It served only as motivation.

References

What we built on

  1. Jigsaw Unintended Bias in Toxicity Classification. Kaggle / Conversation AI, 2019. Our dataset.
  2. Qishen Ha. “8th Place Solution (4 models simple avg).” Jigsaw competition write-up, Kaggle, 2019. The simple-average ensemble we build the Stage 4 argument on.
  3. H. Inoue. “Multi-Sample Dropout for Accelerated Training and Better Generalization.” arXiv:1905.09788, 2019.
  4. V. Sanh, L. Debut, J. Chaumond, T. Wolf. “DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter.” 2019. The backbone we fine-tuned.
  5. A. Vaswani et al. “Attention Is All You Need.” NeurIPS, 2017. The transformer / self-attention.
  6. G. Hinton, O. Vinyals, J. Dean. “Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network.” 2015. What knowledge distillation actually is.
  7. S. Hochreiter, J. Schmidhuber. “Long Short-Term Memory.” Neural Computation, 1997. The LSTM.
  8. A. Krogh, J. Vedelsby. “Neural Network Ensembles, Cross Validation, and Active Learning.” NeurIPS, 1995. The ambiguity decomposition behind ensembling.
  9. S. Khanuja et al. “MuRIL: Multilingual Representations for Indian Languages.” 2021. The RAVEN-X backbone.
  10. B. Mathew et al. “HateXplain: A Benchmark Dataset for Explainable Hate Speech Detection.” AAAI, 2021. The English word-level rationales.
  11. S. Das, A. Dutta, K. Roy, A. Mondal, A. Mukhopadhyay. “A Survey on Automatic Online Hate Speech Detection in Low-Resource Languages.” arXiv:2411.19017, 2024. The supervisor’s survey that set the direction.
A note on honesty. The RNN/classical results and the DistilBERT results were measured under two different evaluation set-ups and are not directly comparable; the larger-transformer and the ensemble are reasoning plus published results, not numbers we have run ourselves yet; and the English DistilBERT score is not a baseline for the Hindi RAVEN-X work. These distinctions are marked throughout with measured literature proposed tags.

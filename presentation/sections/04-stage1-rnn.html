STAGE 1 · CLASSICAL & RNN BASELINES

Stage 1 — Classical and RNN models (the LSTM family)

This was the first modelling stage on the English Jigsaw task. The aim was to set a baseline before trying anything heavier. We built a bag-of-words classifier and then a family of recurrent networks, all on the same data.

Honest caveat. These classical and RNN baselines were primarily a teammate's contribution (Debangshu Dey). We use them here as a comparison family. The DistilBERT transformer and the product stack are the author's (Niladri Hazra) track. We do not claim the author trained the LSTMs.

(a) What we built

All RNN models used the same input setup: a Keras tokenizer with a vocabulary of 100,000, sequences padded or cut to a maximum length of 300, and pretrained word embeddings of shape 276,289 × 300. These embeddings are context-free: each word has one fixed 300-d vector, the same vector no matter where the word appears.

  • TF-IDF + Logistic Regression — a bag-of-words classifier (SGD, log-loss, alpha = 1e-5).
  • Single LSTM (128 units, 10 epochs).
  • BiLSTM — two BiLSTM layers of 128 units, global max pooling, dense head.
  • Weighted BiLSTM — the same with class weights to fight imbalance.
  • BiGRU + Attention (64 units, attention).
  • BiGRU-Conv1D + Attention (64 units, attention, plus a Conv1D layer).

(c) The mathematical reason the RNN approach is limited

The limits below are general theory literature, not specific to our run.

h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t)
path length between token i and token j = O(|i − j|) steps
→ a signal from token i to token j passes through |i − j| multiplications
→ repeated products shrink or grow → vanishing / exploding gradients
  • Sequential recurrence. The hidden state is computed step by step. The path between two tokens grows with their distance, so a long-range dependency must survive many matrix multiplications. LSTM gating (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997) softens this but does not remove it. literature
  • No parallelism over the sequence. Because step t needs step t−1, the network must unroll one token at a time, so training and inference are slow. literature
  • Context-free embeddings. One fixed vector per word. "kill" in "you killed it!" (praise) and "kill" in a threat start from the same vector, so the RNN must separate them from a weak signal. literature
  • Information bottleneck. The whole left context is squeezed into a single fixed-size hidden vector. literature

(d) The drawback we actually saw

These are our measured numbers measured by us.

  • High accuracy hides low toxic recall. On the imbalanced set, TF-IDF+LogReg has a toxic recall of about 0.448 and the Single LSTM about 0.452. They miss more than half of toxic comments while still reading as roughly "94% accurate" on imbalanced data. BiLSTM reaches a toxic recall of about 0.914, but at a much lower validation accuracy (0.8114).
  • Diminishing returns from architecture tweaks. Making the model bidirectional, then weighted, then attention-augmented only moves the Bias-AUC from about 0.89 up to about 0.92. The extra machinery does not break past this band.
Takeaway. The ceiling here is the representation, not the wiring. Context-free word vectors plus a sequential, fixed-size bottleneck cap what these models can learn, and stacking more recurrent layers on top of the same embeddings gives little back. This is what motivates a contextual, attention-based, pretrained transformer.

(e) What it motivates

The next stage replaces context-free vectors with context-dependent ones and replaces the sequential bottleneck with attention, so every token can reach every other token in one step. That is Stage 2: fine-tuning a pretrained transformer (DistilBERT).
